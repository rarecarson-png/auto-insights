Bar graph — all 16 models

The bar graph compares original holdout MAE for all sixteen fixed configurations. Extra Trees has the lowest MAE ($5,575), while Random Forest has lower RMSE ($12,498) and higher R² (0.932). Selection was made using training cross-validation, not this holdout ranking. The comparison supports a strong ensemble baseline but does not establish the best possible tuned algorithm.

Repeated-split data · Original metrics

Box plot — variation across group splits

The box plot summarizes MAE over ten additional family-disjoint holdouts for five configurations. Extra Trees has mean MAE $6,663 and split standard deviation $900. The distributions overlap, so a favorable average does not imply that it wins on every split. Overlapping holdouts also make these observations dependent; the boxes are descriptive, not independent-sample significance tests.

Line graph — sensitivity to held-out families

Each line connects results for split seeds 0–9. These are experimental partition settings, not epochs or chronological observations. The lines show that which vehicle families are held out affects every model. Changes in relative ordering support repeating the evaluation rather than relying on the original seed-42 split.

Scatter plot — actual versus predicted MSRP

Each point is one of the 2,324 held-out configurations evaluated by the selected Extra Trees model. The dashed identity line indicates exact prediction. Logarithmic axes make both inexpensive and expensive configurations visible. Departures from that line show that the aggregate R² of 0.922 does not mean uniformly precise estimates, particularly at the high-price end.

Heatmap — model by experimental partition

The heatmap presents the same ten-holdout MAE observations as a model-by-seed matrix. Lower values indicate smaller dollar errors. Columns reveal the shared difficulty of particular held-out vehicle populations; rows permit within-model comparisons across splits. This view distinguishes variation caused by the partition from variation caused by the chosen configuration.

Residual plot — systematic prediction errors

Positive residuals mean that the model underpredicted recorded MSRP. The widening spread and large positive errors for expensive vehicles expose a limitation hidden by the overall MAE. For vehicles above $100,000, MAE is $43,656, compared with $1,269 for the group at or below $25,000. This supports subgroup diagnostics and future prediction-interval evaluation.
